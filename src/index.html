Details on hipinfer

C++, HIP/CUDA. GitHub, site, Colab2026 to present

  • Built an LLM inference engine in HIP with every kernel written from scratch (no cuBLAS or vendor libraries), supporting dense and MoE models, multi-GPU tensor parallelism, int8 weights, speculative decoding and KV-cache eviction. Tested on NVIDIA; compiles for AMD.
  • Verified against Hugging Face: WikiText-2 perplexity within 0.01% on every model, 99.4 to 99.9% top-1 token agreement, a greedy token-match gate, and kernel tests against PyTorch.
  • Beat llama.cpp at single-request decode on every model tested on 3 GPUs (5 to 16% on 1B to 3B models on a 2080 Ti, at 81 to 87% of memory bandwidth); 23 to 31% faster than vLLM on an A100; GEMM at 92 to 95% of cuBLAS on Turing.
  • 1.5 to 1.7x faster decode on two RTX 2080 Tis with tensor parallelism and a custom all-reduce 1.4x faster than NCCL on small messages; runs 7B to 8B models (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) too large for one card.
  • 1.6 to 1.8x faster decode than fp16 with int8 weights (16 to 26% faster than llama.cpp’s Q8_0, for 0.04 to 0.23% more perplexity).
  • Up to 1.39x faster decode with speculative decoding (1B draft, 3B target), its output token-for-token identical to plain greedy decoding in all 105 test runs on each of 2 GPUs.

Details on SubCache

Python, PyTorch. GitHub, site2026

  • 2.8x higher throughput from keeping 25% of the KV cache on long coding-agent sessions (3x more requests per GPU, for a 5% rise in perplexity), measured in my open-source compression benchmark.
  • Benchmarked 9 eviction policies against H2O and R-KV on agent sessions, LongBench, needle-in-a-haystack retrieval and MATH-500.
  • My submodular eviction method beat H2O by 1.1 to 2.5% on repetitive agent sessions. Profiling showed selection took 71% of decode time, so I wrote GPU kernels for it in hipinfer, 6 to 25x faster than PyTorch.
